## Research: understanding the landscape

Understanding an optimization problem starts with understanding its landscape. In Euclidean spaces, derivatives often reveal this geometry. In infinite-dimensional spaces, functional and geometric inequalities can play a similar role. I like to study the rigidity and quantitative stability encoded by these inequalities, and how they help us understand variational problems and their numerical schemes.

## From Rio to Paris

Before joining ENSAE/CREST, I was a postdoctoral researcher in the [Optimal Transportation team](https://sites.google.com/view/ot-lagrange/) at the Lagrange Mathematical and Computational Research Center, led by G. Carlier, A. Figalli, Q. Mérigot, and F. Santambrogio.

I completed my PhD at **Université Paris-Dauphine** and the [Mokaplan Inria team](https://team.inria.fr/mokaplan/team-members/), supervised by Antonin Chambolle and Vincent Duval. Before that, I studied at **EMAp FGV** in Rio de Janeiro under the guidance of Maria Soledad Aronna.
